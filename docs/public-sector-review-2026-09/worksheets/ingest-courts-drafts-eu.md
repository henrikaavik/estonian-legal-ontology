# Ingest review — courts, drafts, EU (public-sector lens)

## Scope & method

Files read in full: `src/estleg/generate_court_decisions.py` (1954 ln),
`generate_lower_court_decisions.py` (300), `generate_draft_legislation.py` (724),
`generate_eu_legislation.py` (1229), `generate_eu_court_decisions.py` (927),
`eurlex_common.py` (94), `link_curia_eu_legislation.py` (316),
`rederive_riigikohus_case_types.py` (257), `normalize_court_referenced_law.py` (223).
Context: `AGENTS.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`.

Evidence gathered from the committed corpus with `python3`/`jq` over
`krr_outputs/{riigikohus,kohtud,eelnoud,eurlex,curia}/`, plus a wiring pass over
`scripts/run_all_integration.py`, `.github/workflows/`, `shacl/`, `tests/`,
`metadata.jsonld`, `NOTICE`, `docs/DATA_PROTECTION.md`.

Corpus scale confirmed: 12,104 Riigikohus decisions; 3 lower-court decisions;
22,832 drafts; 33,242 EU acts; 22,290 EU court decisions.

Review lens: can a Estonian public body trust, operate, integrate and lawfully
reuse these five feeds.

---

## Strengths

**EU ingest is genuinely well engineered.** `eurlex_common.py:27-60` documents and
enforces POST-not-GET against the Publications Office Virtuoso endpoint, with an
explicit HTTP-202 guard; `eurlex_common.py:63-94` adds bounded exponential
backoff. Both EU generators paginate with `ORDER BY ?work`
(`generate_eu_legislation.py:269`, `generate_eu_court_decisions.py:522`), so page
boundaries are stable across runs. `--allow-partial` records `partial: true` in
the index and exits 2 (`generate_eu_legislation.py:1220-1225`,
`generate_eu_court_decisions.py:919-923`) — a truncated sweep is visible to an
operator instead of silently shipping.

**Identifier hygiene on the EU side is close to what an EU-level consumer needs.**
`generate_eu_legislation.py:588-610` emits `owl:sameAs` to both the CELLAR CELEX
PSI and the ELI canonical URI, and `eli_natural_id` (`:526-539`) puts the real ELI
natural id (`reg/2010/288`) in `eli:id_local` rather than the CELEX.
`generate_eu_court_decisions.py:708-713` does the same for CELEX + percent-encoded
ECLI PSI. ECLI coverage on the EU corpus is 22,225/22,290 (99.7%).

**Date fidelity is defended.** `_is_valid_iso_date`
(`generate_eu_legislation.py:495-512`) rejects CELLAR's `1001-01-01`/`1002-02-02`
null sentinels; `generate_eu_court_decisions.py:744-756` bounds case-law years to
[1950, now+1]. Malformed dates are skipped and logged rather than emitted as
invalid `xsd:date`.

**CELEX classification is careful and honest.** `classify_eu_doc_type`
(`generate_eu_legislation.py:117-145`) refuses to type a sector-4/5 CELEX as a
Regulation and drops the record rather than mistyping it;
`generate_eu_court_decisions.py:509-515` filters sector-8 (national) case law out
of the CJEU corpus, and `UNKNOWN_CELEX_CODES` is surfaced in `CURIA_INDEX.json`
(`:882`) so new EUR-Lex type codes are visible.

**Case-number classification carries real domain knowledge.**
`generate_court_decisions.py:169-217` correctly separates the 1996–2017
`3-<chamber>-…` layout (where the leading `3` is the *court*, not the case type)
from the 2018+ `<type>-<yy>-…` layout, and from the 1993–1996 Roman
`III-<chamber>/…` form. `_MODERN_YEAR_RE` is anchored (`:673`) so the third
segment of a legacy number cannot be misread as a filing year. Only 89 of 12,104
decisions remain `CaseType_Other`.

**`rederive_riigikohus_case_types.py` is a model of conservative enrichment.** It
retypes only from a single unambiguous chamber token
(`:137-157`), uses a head-only tiebreak so body cross-references cannot retype the
case (`:89-104`), refuses to guess when the signal is ambiguous, is idempotent,
and skips unmaterialised LFS pointers (`:112-118`).

**Deduplication is content-aware, not just IRI-aware.** `_decision_content_key`
(`generate_court_decisions.py:1012-1027`) catches the rikos behaviour where one
real document is returned under several `object_id`s, and
`dedupe_court_decision_graph` (`:1060-1109`) returns a remap so dangling refs are
rewritten rather than left pointing at a dropped node.

**Rights and privacy are documented rather than hand-waved.** `NOTICE:36-93`
states upstream terms per source; `LICENSE:1-16` explicitly refuses to license the
corpus under MIT; `docs/DATA_PROTECTION.md` names the three personal-data
subcorpora and `metadata.jsonld:420,438` carries
`estleg:containsPersonalData: true`. Most projects at this stage have none of this.

## Weaknesses / risks

### W1 — The Riigikohus corpus cannot be regenerated by its own generator (severity: CRITICAL)

The committed corpus and the current code disagree on almost every emitted shape.
Measured over all 12,104 decisions:

| Check | Current generator emits | Corpus contains |
|---|---|---|
| `@id` = `RK_<case>_<rikObjectId>` (`:988-1009`) | always | 121 of 12,104 |
| `rdfs:label` (`:1200`) | `{"@value",…,"@language":"et"}` | plain string, 12,104/12,104 |
| `estleg:summary` (`:1230`) | `{"@value",…,"@language":"et"}` | plain string, 12,104/12,104 |
| `dcterms:source` (`:1244`) | set whenever `link` present | 0 nodes |

So `python3 -m estleg.generate_court_decisions` today would **remint ~11,983 of
12,104 decision IRIs**, change two literals from `rdf:langString` to `xsd:string`,
and add a `dcterms:source` the corpus has never had. Every
`estleg:interpretsLaw` / citation edge pointing at `estleg:RK_*` would dangle.
The IRI scheme is not on the ARCHITECTURE.md "do not change without a MAJOR
version" list (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:106-113`), yet it is load-bearing.

For a public body the consequence is blunt: there is no runnable path from
rikos.rik.ee to the shipped corpus. It is effectively a hand-curated artefact.

### W2 — Every ingest generator is full-overwrite; enrichment lives in the same files (severity: CRITICAL)

`generate_court_decisions.main()` builds `graph` from scratch (`:1867-1903`) and
`save_json`s over `riigikohus_<year>_peep.json`. `decision_to_node` only reads
`legal_text` off the fetched row (`:1253`), and `fetch_year`/`_extract_row_fields`
(`:156-185`) never set it. A re-scrape therefore deletes:

- `estleg:legalText` on 10,940 decisions (the multi-hour `--fetch-full-text` run),
- `estleg:chamber` (9,786), `estleg:judge` (10,833),
- `estleg:interpretsLaw` (9,162), `interpretsVersion`, `interpretationOutdated`,
  `earliestSupersedingDate` — the whole court↔provision join,
- the `rederive_riigikohus_case_types.py` retypes and the
  `normalize_court_referenced_law.py` normalisation.

Same bug class elsewhere:

- `generate_eu_legislation.py:1084-1092` rewrites `eurlex_*_peep.json` from
  fetched rows; `legislation_to_node` never emits `estleg:transposedBy`, so a
  re-fetch destroys all 206 directive→Estonian-law transposition edges. Only the
  boolean `estoniaRelevant` survives, because `collect_estonia_relevant_celex`
  (`:743-777`) reads the CELEX set before the overwrite.
- `generate_eu_court_decisions.py:832-838` rewrites `curia_*_peep.json`;
  `decision_to_node` never emits `estleg:interpretsEULaw`, so a re-fetch destroys
  all 4,559 edges written by `link_curia_eu_legislation.py`.

`rebuild_eurlex_combined_from_peeps` (`generate_eu_legislation.py:886-940`) exists
precisely because combined and peeps drift. It patches one symptom of this design.

The architectural root cause: ingest is *replace*, enrichment is *mutate in
place*, and both target the same files, with no merge step and no separation of a
raw ingest layer from a derived overlay layer.

### W3 — Personal data is in every load surface, and nothing screens it (severity: CRITICAL for public-sector adoption)

`estleg:summary` is present on 12,104 of 12,104 Riigikohus decisions and is
written straight from the rikos search table (`:1229-1230`). Sample from
`krr_outputs/riigikohus/riigikohus_2020_peep.json`, node `estleg:RK_3_18_1432_93`:

```
"estleg:summary": "Egon Tischleri kassatsioonkaebus Tallinna Ringkonnakohtu
09.09.2019. a otsuse peale …"
```

`estleg:legalText` (10,940 nodes, `:1701`) carries the full decision body. Measured
over the corpus:

| Finding | Count |
|---|---|
| Decisions whose `legalText` contains an 11-digit Estonian personal ID pattern | 42 |
| …of those, the digits directly follow the word "isikukood" | 21 |
| Decisions with a full name immediately after süüdistatav/hageja/kaebaja | ~405 |

At least one 2007-era decision pseudonymises the name to initials but leaves the
`isikukood` and a home address in plaintext.

`docs/DATA_PROTECTION.md` is honest about this and is explicitly marked DRAFT with
"DO NOT redact yet, FLAG only". But there is **no code anywhere in `src/` or
`scripts/` that redacts, masks or screens personal data** before writing. Note the
pipeline does not merely mirror RIK's anonymisation — it republishes it verbatim,
which makes the project an independent controller under GDPR (as `docs/DATA_RIGHTS.md:97-109`
itself states). Article 10 (criminal convictions) applies to the criminal subset.

A plaintext `isikukood` is the specific item that will stop Justiitsministeerium,
Riigikohus or AKI from adopting this feed, regardless of the notice.

### W4 — `krr_outputs/kohtud/` is a 3-row placeholder published as a load surface (severity: HIGH)

`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:37` lists `kohtud` in `PUBLIC_LOAD_SUBDIRS` — the "Full
public RDF" product. The directory contains 3 decisions.

`main()` fetches exactly one page: `search_payload(page=0, …)`
(`generate_lower_court_decisions.py:284-286`). There is no pagination loop at all,
and `--limit` defaults to 50 (`:271`). Two of the three committed object ids are
`200000001` and `200000002` — sequential synthetic values next to a real
`108397986`. `KOHTUD_INDEX.json` has one commit in its entire history.

The index is honest (`api_total` vs `ingested`), but the *graph* is not: for a
`--year` run the ontology header reads "Esimese ja teise astme kohtulahendid
2026" (`:233-238`) with no sample qualifier, so a SPARQL consumer asking "how many
Harju Maakohus decisions cite TsMS §?" gets a confident, tiny, wrong answer.

Also lost: `file_id`, `file_name` and `status` are parsed (`:126-130`) and never
emitted; there is no ECLI at all for lower courts (Estonian first/second-instance
decisions do carry ECLI); `decision_date` prefers `staatusKp` (a status-change
date) over `lahendiKuulutamiseAeg` (the pronouncement date) at `:97`, which stamps
the wrong date as `estleg:decisionDate`; and `sort: "new-first"` (`:188`) with a
full overwrite makes output nondeterministic, against the AGENTS.md rule.

### W5 — Nothing schedules the ingests, and one has a hardcoded end year (severity: HIGH)

`scripts/run_all_integration.py` is an *enrichment* DAG. Of the eight modules in
scope, it invokes only `generate_eu_legislation --rebuild-combined-from-peeps` and
`link_curia_eu_legislation`. The six real ingests are operator-run.
`.github/workflows/validate.yml` runs weekly but explicitly does not fetch.

`generate_court_decisions.main()` hardcodes `start_year = 1993; end_year = 2026`
(`:1836-1837`). In January 2027 the scraper silently stops collecting new
decisions with no error.

`metadata.jsonld:261` declares `accrualPeriodicity: monthly` for the dataset, and
`scripts/check_rt_staleness.py` guards Riigi Teataja with a 45-day budget. There
is **no cadence statement and no staleness canary for riigikohus, kohtud, eelnoud,
eurlex or curia.** A ministry cannot tell how old the court or draft data is.

### W6 — `fetch_year` fails a whole year silently, and the run still exits 0 (severity: HIGH)

`fetch_year` (`:313-347`) has no retry on the search pages — unlike the detail-page
fetcher, which has three attempts with backoff (`:1513-1531`). A single 5xx on page
7 of 12 raises, and `main()`'s `except Exception: print("ERROR"); continue`
(`:1856-1860`) drops the entire year. The year file on disk is left untouched, the
year is missing from `year_stats`, `RIIGIKOHUS_INDEX.json` is rewritten without
it, and the process exits 0. An operator running this in cron would see success.

Compare the EU generators, which get this right with `--allow-partial` +
`partial: true` + exit 2.

### W7 — The Estonia-relevance lens is unusable as a scope filter (severity: HIGH)

`estleg:estoniaRelevant` is stamped `true` only for acts whose CELEX appears in
`transposition_mapping.json` or already carries `transposedBy`
(`generate_eu_legislation.py:743-777`), and non-relevant nodes deliberately omit
the property rather than carrying `false` (`:780-794`).

Measured: 206 nodes of 33,242 (0.6%), **all directives, no regulation ever**.

Three problems for the stated public-sector use case:
1. It means "we found an Estonian transposition", not "relevant to Estonia".
   Absence conflates "not relevant" with "not yet mapped" — the 2,908 directives
   with no mapping are indistinguishable from irrelevant ones.
2. EU regulations are directly applicable in Estonia and are arguably the most
   relevant acts of all; none can ever carry the flag.
3. A ministry filtering `?act estleg:estoniaRelevant true` sees 0.6% of the
   acquis and concludes the corpus is empty.

`EURLEX_INDEX.json`'s `lens` block advertises this as "the in-force /
Estonia-pertinent consumer cut (#527)" (`:835-838`), which overstates it.

### W8 — Transposition monitoring is not actionable (severity: HIGH for Justiitsministeerium)

`estleg:transpositionDeadline` is present on 2,599 of 3,114 directives, correctly
guarded to directives only (`:635-647`) and taking the earliest of multiple
corrigenda values (`:302-309`). That is the right raw material and it is missing
the three things a transposition monitor needs:

- **status** — no `transposed` / `partially transposed` / `overdue` derivation,
  no comparison of deadline against `BUILD_EVALUATION_DATE`,
- **the implementing act** — `transposedBy` covers 206 of 3,114 directives (6.6%),
- **the national implementing measure (NIM)** — CELLAR exposes Member-State NIM
  declarations; they are not queried here.

A 1981 deadline on a repealed directive is also indistinguishable from a live
obligation unless the consumer separately joins `estleg:inForce`.

### W9 — Draft phase is the *earliest* feed observed, not the current stage (severity: HIGH)

`estleg:legislativePhase` is documented as "The current legislative phase of the
draft" (`generate_draft_legislation.py:412`). It is actually derived from which
RSS feed the item was first seen in. `main()` iterates `RSS_FEEDS` in dict order —
publicConsultation, review, submission — and dedupes on first sight
(`:585-588`). A draft that went through consultation *and* review *and* submission
is recorded under **PublicConsultation**.

The corpus shows the effect: 6 drafts from 2011 and 18 from 2012 are still typed
`Phase_PublicConsultation`. The whole `publicconsultation` bucket is 121 drafts
spanning 2011–2026 — roughly eight per year, which is not a credible picture of
Estonian public consultation activity, whereas review holds 13,665 and submission
7,995 over the same period.

### W10 — A draft cannot be followed to enactment (severity: HIGH for Riigikogu / ministry draft tracking)

`Phase_Enacted`, `Phase_Withdrawn` and `Phase_Rejected` are declared as
`eli-dl:ProcessStage` individuals (`:308-331`) and **no draft points at any of
them** — confirmed 0/0/0 across all 22,832 nodes. The three EIS RSS feeds only
cover executive-branch stages; once a bill leaves the government for Riigikogu,
EIS stops publishing and this pipeline has no Riigikogu source
(`api.riigikogu.ee` / `eelnoud.riigikogu.ee` are not used, and `riigikogu.ee` is
not on the HTTP allowlist at `estleg_common.py:1981-1993`).

There is also no reading-stage modelling (I/II/III lugemine), no
`eli-dl:ProcessStep` with dates, and no ELI-DL Work/Expression/Manifestation
split — the ELI-DL alignment is a single `rdfs:subClassOf` (`:258`) plus stage
individuals. It would not validate as ELI-DL for the Publications Office.

Inconsistency worth noting: 133 drafts carry `estleg:enactedAs` while none carry
an Enacted phase.

### W11 — Derived edges carry no provenance or confidence marker (severity: MEDIUM-HIGH, cross-cutting)

Nothing in the graph distinguishes an authoritative value from a heuristic one:

- `estleg:ecliIdentifier` on Riigikohus decisions is **minted**, not sourced:
  `mint_riigikohus_ecli` (`:772-801`) builds `ECLI:EE:RK:<year>:<case with -,/ → .>`
  for every decision after 2016-07-01. It is never checked against Riigi Teataja's
  own ECLI register, and `rdfs:seeAlso` links to two resolvers (`:811-825`) are
  emitted without verifying they resolve. Coverage is 2,903 of 12,104.
- `estleg:interpretsEULaw` is parsed out of Estonian *title text* with regexes
  (`link_curia_eu_legislation.py:39-126`) and a synthesised CELEX
  (`_celex`, `:99-100`, which hardcodes sector `3`), then kept only if the target
  node exists. CELLAR already publishes the authoritative citation relation
  (`cdm:case-law_cites_legal_resource`); it is not queried. Recall is 4,559 of
  22,290 decisions (20.5%).
- `estleg:caseType` written by `rederive_riigikohus_case_types.py:179` from
  decision text is indistinguishable from one derived from the case number.
- `estleg:draftType` (`generate_draft_legislation.py:122-167`) is a title-substring
  `elif` chain emitted as a hard object property. `DraftType_Other` is the single
  largest bucket at 9,334 of 22,832 (41%).

A Riigikohus or ministry lawyer cannot ask "is this the official ECLI or your
guess?" from the data.

### W12 — Court citations that fail to resolve are deleted (severity: MEDIUM-HIGH)

`normalize_court_referenced_law.py:70-84` drops any `referencedLaw` value that is
neither a known abbreviation nor in `FULLNAME_GENITIVE`. Its own docstring names
the casualties: `kalapüügiseaduse`, `jahiseaduse` (`:22-24`). When the list
empties, the key is removed entirely (`:125-126, 153`).

The result is a silent false negative: a decision that genuinely cites the Fishing
Act now asserts nothing. `estleg:referencedLaw` coverage is 1,630 of 12,104
decisions. The unresolvable raw token is a lead for a lawyer and a gap report for
a maintainer; deleting it destroys both. This is registry incompleteness being
laundered into apparent data cleanliness.

Note also that `detect_referenced_laws` reads only `estleg:summary`
(`generate_court_decisions.py:1265`), never `estleg:legalText`, so citations are
mined from an 800-character abstract while the full body sits unused on the same
node.

### W13 — Silent truncation without markers (severity: MEDIUM)

- Court summary truncated to 800 chars (`generate_court_decisions.py:1230`).
- EU act titles truncated to 500 chars, both ET and EN
  (`generate_eu_legislation.py:564-565`). EU act titles routinely exceed this.
- EU court decision labels truncated to 500 with an ellipsis
  (`generate_eu_court_decisions.py:460-470`) — `dcterms:title` keeps the full
  value here, which is the right pattern and is *not* applied to the two above.

No `estleg:truncated` flag, no `dcterms:extent`. A consumer comparing an act title
against CELLAR sees a mismatch with no explanation.

### W14 — Freshness and index self-consistency drift (severity: MEDIUM)

`RIIGIKOHUS_INDEX.json` reports `decisions_with_full_text: 10941` against an
actual 10,940, and `10941 + 1196 = 12137` against `total_decisions: 12104` — a
33-decision drift, because the full-text pass and the index snapshot ran against
different populations. `EELNOUD_INDEX.json` and `CURIA_INDEX.json` are dated
2026-06-30 while their sibling peeps are 2026-08-19.

There is no gate asserting index totals equal graph node counts for these five
corpora.

### W15 — Nondeterminism from wall-clock reads (severity: MEDIUM)

`generate_draft_legislation.py:553` bounds `publicationDate` with
`datetime.now().year + 1`; `generate_eu_court_decisions.py:747` bounds
`documentDate` with `datetime.now().year + 1`. The same inputs produce different
output across a New Year boundary. `BUILD_EVALUATION_DATE`
(`estleg_common.py:1549`) exists for exactly this and is used for the `generated`
stamps but not for these bounds.

`UNKNOWN_CELEX_CODES` (`generate_eu_court_decisions.py:155`) is a module-level
mutable global written as a side effect of `classify_from_celex`, making the
classifier impure and the index field dependent on call history.

### W16 — No test would catch an upstream format change (severity: MEDIUM)

- rikos.rik.ee HTML: `test_row_with_wrong_column_count_is_skipped`
  (`tests/test_generate_court_decisions.py:301`) tests the parser against
  synthetic HTML, not a captured fixture, and CI never fetches.
- EIS RSS: every test monkeypatches `fetch_rss` itself
  (`tests/test_generate_draft_legislation.py:161,348,420,485`); the real XML/title
  parser at `generate_draft_legislation.py:214-248` is never exercised.
- EUR-Lex SPARQL shape: only a self-fabricated `_FakeResp`
  (`tests/test_generate_eu_sources.py:533`).
- Zero CELLAR rows: `test_curia_pagination_query_is_ordered` asserts empty results
  are *accepted* as normal pagination termination.

An upstream HTML or RSS change surfaces as an empty or partial corpus, not a test
failure.

### W17 — Draft affected-law extraction is low-coverage and noisy (severity: MEDIUM)

`estleg:affectedLawName` is present on 2,344 of 22,832 drafts (10.3%). 377 values
exceed 80 characters, up to 238; one (`Draft_REM25_1323`) begins mid-sentence.
The `([\w.]+(?:\s+\w+)*?\s+seadus)\b` pattern
(`generate_draft_legislation.py:191-196`) also leaves the truncated stem alongside
the full title, e.g. `["Riigi Teataja seaduse", "Riigi Teataja seaduse muutmise seadus"]`
in `estleg:Draft_JDM26_0214`.

`estleg:initiator` is a plain string (`:533`) rather than an IRI into the
`institutions/` subcorpus the repo already ships, so drafts cannot be joined to
ministries in SPARQL. `MINISTRY_CODES` (`:76-90`) has no pre-2024 `JUM` entry and
maps `REM` to "Regionaalminister" (a minister, not the ministry).

### W18 — `estleg:judge` publishes panel names with no schema or SHACL cover (severity: MEDIUM)

`extract_judges` (`:962-973`) writes 10,833 nodes' worth of judge names.
`estleg:judge` is the one court predicate constrained by **no** SHACL shape (all
of `courtName`, `courtLevel`, `courtKind`, `rtObjektId`, `chamber`,
`ecliIdentifier` are covered by `CourtDecisionShape`). Extraction quality is
visibly imperfect: `estleg:RK_III_2_3_9_93` holds
`["J. Odar liikmed: T. Kollom", "T. Vernik"]` — the role word "liikmed:" fused
into the name. Judges are public officials so the GDPR exposure is low, but the
data is published unvalidated.

### W19 — Small efficiency and correctness residue (severity: LOW)

- `generate_eu_court_decisions.py:542-552` merges authors with a linear scan over
  a growing 22k list — O(n²). The identical bug was fixed in
  `generate_eu_legislation.py:294` by switching to a `by_celex` dict; the fix was
  not carried across.
- `decision_to_node(dec, year, …)` (`generate_court_decisions.py:1146`) never uses
  its `year` parameter.
- `generate_draft_legislation.py:12` documents outputs as `eelnou_*.json`; the code
  writes `eelnoud_<phase>_peep.json` (`:649`).
- `generate_eu_court_decisions.py:828` sets `dc:source: "EUR-Lex / CURIA"`, but
  nothing is fetched from curia.europa.eu — the host is not even on the allowlist.
  The directory name and docstring mislead about provenance.
- `estleg:euCaseNumber` is emitted as a list for joined cases (`:722-727`) while
  `EUCourtDecisionShape` still pins `sh:maxCount 1` — flagged in the code comment
  at `:717-720` as a deferred break that will fire on the next regen.

### W20 — No Estonian-language documentation exists (severity: MEDIUM for adoption)

All 8 root `*.md` and all 17 `docs/*.md` are English. The only Estonian prose in
the repo is the corpus's own legal text and one `@language: "et"` description in
`metadata.jsonld:24-27`. Riigi Teataja, RIK, Õiguskantsler and KOV legal staff
work in Estonian; a governance decision to adopt this feed needs an Estonian
one-pager on provenance, coverage, freshness and the personal-data position.

## Improvement ideas

**1. Split ingest from enrichment: raw layer + overlay layer.**
*What:* Have each ingest generator write `krr_outputs/<corpus>/raw/` only, and move
every derived predicate (`legalText`, `chamber`, `judge`, `interpretsLaw`,
`interpretsEULaw`, `transposedBy`, retyped `caseType`, normalised `referencedLaw`)
into sidecar overlay files merged at publish time — the pattern already used for
`sanctions/`, `concepts/`, `annotations/`. Alternatively, make each generator
merge with the on-disk graph by `@id` instead of replacing it.
*Why:* Today a re-scrape destroys the multi-hour full-text run, 9,162 court→provision
edges, 4,559 EU citation edges and 206 transposition edges (W2). No public body
can operate a pipeline whose refresh step deletes its most valuable content. This
is the precondition for every other freshness improvement below.
*Effort:* L. *Impact:* H.

## Open questions

1. **Which Riigikohus IRI scheme is canonical?** 12,104 shipped nodes use
   `RK_<case>`; the code mints `RK_<case>_<oid>`. Answering this determines whether
   improvement 2 is a code fix or a corpus migration.
2. **Was the committed Riigikohus corpus produced by an earlier version of this
   generator, or partly by hand?** `dcterms:source` on 0 nodes and plain-string
   labels/summaries suggest divergence I could not reconstruct from git history in
   the time available.
3. **What is the intended legal position on republishing named parties?**
   `docs/DATA_PROTECTION.md` is DRAFT with "DO NOT redact yet". Improvement 3
   deliberately covers only ID codes because the name question is a policy call,
   not an engineering one. Has AKI or a DPO been consulted?
4. **Is `krr_outputs/kohtud/` intended to become a full ingest, or is it a
   permanent illustrative sample?** Improvement 4 branches on this.
5. **Does the EIS `publicConsult.rss` feed genuinely list only ~8 consultations a
   year, or is the corpus missing a paginated endpoint?** 121 items over 2011–2026
   against 13,665 in review looks like an upstream limitation, but I could not
   verify without fetching.
6. **Do the minted `ECLI:EE:RK:YYYY:<dotted case>` values match Riigi Teataja's
   own ECLI register?** The format is plausible but unverified; a 20-decision
   spot-check against `riigiteataja.ee/kohtulahendid/ecli/` would settle it.
7. **Is `estleg:decisionLink` (`riigikohus.ee/et/lahendid/?asjaNr=…`) a live
   document URL or a search page that may not resolve?** `estleg:rikosUrl` is the
   object-id document URL and looks more reliable; the two are never
   cross-validated.
8. **Is EUR-Lex/CELLAR the intended long-term source for the `curia/` corpus?**
   Nothing is fetched from curia.europa.eu, which is a strength operationally but
   means the directory name, docstring and `dc:source` all misdescribe provenance.
